Real-Time Control of Additive Manufacturing

Overview

This project connects the quality requirements for a returned component with the commands sent to the deposition process. The aim is to plan the repair from the measured damage and then adjust the process as the plant deposits new material.

From repair requirements to machine commands

The quality control workflow begins with incoming quality control (IQC). It measures the returned part, identifies the repair region, and prepares the scan path and acceptable limits. In-process quality control (IPQC) uses these limits to monitor and adjust the build. Outgoing quality control (OQC) checks the repaired part against its requirements.

The deterministic controller estimates temperature and the shape of the melt pool, the small region of metal melted by the laser. Model predictive control uses the estimate to choose laser power and scan speed for the next several steps. It applies the first command and updates the plan with the next measurement. The assignment uses the ET+RUSLS estimator and an L-BFGS optimizer.

The study also proposes an AI-assisted controller for adapting to process variation. Two recurrent neural networks estimate temperature and melt-pool geometry from recent measurements. A global optimizer searches for suitable commands as the model adapts.

Simulation results

The deterministic controller was tested on a CNN-based surrogate for the titanium alloy Ti-6Al-4V. The target depth was 100–150 µm below the surface, with a temperature limit of 2,200 K. In the simulated heat distribution, the depth trace settles between the dashed limits along the central part of the scan. The controller took 7 ms per update on average and 23 ms at most on the study hardware.

The repair planning example shows how the pre-planner identifies the required scan paths by keeping the portion of each path inside the damaged region. An Inconel 718 finite-element study in Autodesk Netfabb took 1 hour 52 minutes for the 10-layer powder-bed fusion example and 10 hours 47 minutes for the directed energy deposition example. These runs provided detailed offline process simulations. The surrogate-based controller supplied the rapid updates in the melt-pool study.
